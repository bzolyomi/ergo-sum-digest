Is AI conscious, and who benefits from the question?

Thomas Nagel, newly awarded the Berggruen Prize, gave Wired an interview in which, per the quoted snippets, he argues data centers share nothing with the physical character of a nervous system and that language alone is not a natural manifestation of consciousness. Commenters read this as a 'no body, no consciousness' position; I only have excerpts, not the full interview. Mor Naaman and Frank Pasquale argue in Tech Policy Press that AI is an artifact, 'made, not born', and that granting it rights would shift power to those who control it. Others push back on the framing: one post notes that uncertainty about consciousness already covers animals, infants and hypothetical aliens, so questions about humanlike machine minds are not unreasonable. A philpapers item takes a higher-order theory approach, asking what self-knowledge of mental states would mean for AI. The Financial Times piece on whether companies should build consciousness deliberately is known to me only by its teaser.
